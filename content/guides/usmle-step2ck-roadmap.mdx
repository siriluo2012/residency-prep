With Step 1 pass/fail, Step 2 CK is the main numeric signal in a residency application. It tests clinical knowledge and the ability to choose the next best step in diagnosis, management, and prevention.

<ExamFacts exam="usmle-step2ck" />

## Blueprint

<Blueprint exam="usmle-step2ck" />

Clinical disciplines are weighted Medicine 55-65%, Pediatrics 17-27%, Obstetrics and Gynecology 10-20%, Psychiatry 10-15%, and Surgery 5-15%. Physician tasks emphasise Diagnosis (16-20%), Laboratory and Diagnostic Studies (13-17%), Mixed Management (12-16%), and Pharmacotherapy (8-12%).

## How Step 2 CK questions differ from Step 1

- The lead-in is almost always "most appropriate next step", "most likely diagnosis", or "most appropriate pharmacotherapy".
- Vignettes are longer and include vitals, labs, and imaging descriptions; reading speed matters. Practice in timed mode early.
- Distractors are often *reasonable but premature* steps (e.g., imaging before stabilization). Our explanations label the misconception each distractor exploits.
- Ethics, patient safety, and systems-based practice are 10-15% of the exam. Do not skip them.
